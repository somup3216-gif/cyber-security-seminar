01 · Definition

What is Cyber Security?

Cyber security combines technologies, processes, and practices used to protect systems and data from cyber threats. It includes prevention, detection, response, and recovery.

Example: College account

A unique password and multi-factor authentication can help prevent someone else from accessing your student account.

Example: Online payment

Checking the recipient and using a trusted payment app helps reduce the chance of fraud.

Four parts of a security approach

  • Prevent: make attacks harder.
  • Detect: notice suspicious activity.
  • Respond: contain an incident and investigate.
  • Recover: restore services and learn from the event.
02 · Foundation

The CIA Triad

A classic information-security model built around three goals:

🔒

Confidentiality

Information is seen only by authorized people. Examples: access permissions and encryption.

✅

Integrity

Information stays accurate and is not changed improperly. Examples: checksums and controlled editing.

🟢

Availability

Systems and data are ready when authorized users need them. Examples: backups and reliable infrastructure.

Memory trick: C = keep it private · I = keep it correct · A = keep it accessible.
03 · Security areas

Types of Cyber Security

Network Security

Protects network traffic and infrastructure using firewalls, segmentation, and monitoring.

Application Security

Reduces software weaknesses through secure design, testing, and updates.

Information Security

Protects information in any form, including access control and data handling.

Cloud Security

Protects cloud-hosted data, accounts, services, and configurations.

Endpoint Security

Protects laptops, desktops, phones, and other connected devices.

Identity & Access

Ensures users prove who they are and receive only needed permissions.

04 · Risk awareness

Common Cyber Threats

🎣 Phishing

Fake emails, messages, or pages that trick people into revealing data or clicking unsafe links.

Clue: unexpected urgency or a suspicious sender.

🦠 Malware

Malicious software that can spy, damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access.

🔐 Ransomware

Malware that encrypts or locks files and demands payment. Backups and updates help reduce risk.

🔑 Password attacks

Attempts to guess, reuse, or exploit stolen passwords. Unique passwords and MFA help.

🪪 Identity theft

Using someone’s personal information without permission, often for fraud.

🌐 DoS / DDoS

Flooding a service with traffic so legitimate users may not be able to access it.

If a message asks for your password, one-time code, or urgent payment, stop and verify through a separate trusted channel.
05 · Why it matters

Importance of Cyber Security

  • Protects personal, academic, financial, and business information.
  • Helps prevent unauthorized access and data breaches.
  • Reduces financial loss, downtime, and reputational damage.
  • Supports privacy, trust, and safe digital communication.
  • Helps organizations maintain services and meet responsibilities.

Who needs it?

Students, families, colleges, companies, hospitals, banks, and public services—all users of digital systems.

06 · Everyday defense

How to Stay Safe Online

Use strong passwords

Choose long, unique passwords or passphrases. A password manager can help.

Enable MFA / 2FA

Add another verification step, such as an authenticator app or security key.

Verify links

Check the sender and domain. Navigate to important sites yourself instead of trusting message links.

Update devices

Install trusted system and app updates to fix known vulnerabilities.

Back up important files

Keep backups in a separate or protected location and periodically test recovery.

Secure Wi-Fi

Use trusted networks, a strong router password, and avoid sensitive tasks on unknown networks.

Simple rule: Pause before you click. Verify before you share. Report if something seems wrong.

Slide 2 · Definition

Cyber security is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications, and information from unauthorized access, damage, or cyberattacks. It includes prevention, detection, response, and recovery. For example, protecting a college account with a unique password and multi-factor authentication is a simple cyber security practice.

Slide 3 · CIA Triad

Cyber security is based on three important principles called the CIA Triad. Confidentiality means keeping information private. Integrity means keeping data accurate and preventing improper changes. Availability means ensuring that systems and information are accessible when needed. These three principles are the foundation of information security.

Slide 4 · Types

There are different types of cyber security. Network security protects networks. Application security protects software. Information security protects data. Cloud security protects online services and stored information. Endpoint security protects devices such as laptops and smartphones. These areas work together to create stronger protection.

Slide 5 · Threats

Some common cyber threats are phishing, malware, ransomware, password attacks, and identity theft. Phishing uses fake messages or websites to trick people. Malware is harmful software. Ransomware can lock or encrypt data and demand payment. These threats can affect individuals, organizations, and governments.

Slide 6 · Importance

Cyber security is important because it protects personal and financial information, prevents unauthorized access, reduces financial losses, and supports privacy. Our online accounts, banking applications, college records, and personal photos all need protection.

Slide 7 · Safety and conclusion

We can stay safer by using strong and unique passwords, enabling two-factor authentication, avoiding suspicious links, updating software, and backing up important files. Possible Teacher Questions & Answers

1. What is cyber security?

It is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications, and data from cyber threats and unauthorized access.

2. What is the CIA Triad?

It is a security model consisting of Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.

3. What is phishing?

Phishing is a social-engineering attack that uses fake messages or websites to steal information or persuade people to take unsafe actions.

4. What is malware?

Malware is malicious software designed to damage, disrupt, spy on, or gain unauthorized access to systems.

5. What is a firewall?

A firewall monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic based on security rules.

6. What is two-factor authentication?

It is a login method that requires two different verification factors to help confirm a user's identity.

7. What is ransomware?

Ransomware is malware that encrypts or locks data and demands payment for its recovery.

8. Are all hackers cybercriminals?

No. Some hackers work with permission to test and improve security. Cybercriminals use technology for illegal activities.

9. What is the difference between HTTP and HTTPS?

HTTPS uses TLS encryption to protect data in transit between a browser and a website. HTTP does not provide that transport encryption.

I chose cyber security because it is important in computer science and our daily digital lives. I want to learn how to protect information and use technology safely.
